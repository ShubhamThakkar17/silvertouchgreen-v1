01Two energy sources together

Solar and wind do not always generate electricity at the same time or at the same rate.

Solar panels depend on sunlight, while wind turbines generate power when wind conditions are suitable. Combining these sources can help balance their changing output and make better use of the renewable resources available at a site.

02How it works

How does a hybrid solar + wind system work?

A hybrid system uses both solar panels and wind turbines for electricity production from two sources. When there is sunlight, solar panels produce electricity. If the wind is favourable, wind turbines also generate electricity.

24-hour profile · illustrative

The balance changes with the weather

The balance between these sources varies with the location, season and weather. A suitable design considers these differences rather than assuming that both sources will always generate at their maximum capacity.

Where battery storage fits

Battery storage could also be taken into consideration in case the project calls for it. Batteries would be used to store any excess electricity that is generated; however, this depends on the design and storage capabilities of the system. Stored energy can help manage variations in generation, but does not guarantee an uninterrupted power supply.

03Who can benefit

Who can benefit from hybrid energy?

Hybrid solar and wind systems may suit organisations with suitable solar and wind resources, sufficient installation space, and a need to diversify their renewable electricity generation.

Industrial facilities

Sites that have heavy electricity needs and could benefit by integrating renewable power resources.

Commercial establishments

Enterprises investigating other options to traditional electricity supplied by the grid system and where local conditions support both technologies.

Remote and infrastructure sites

Locations where energy access, available resources and operating requirements make a hybrid approach worth assessing.

Suitability depends on

  • Wind speeds
  • Sunlight availability
  • Land conditions
  • Connection requirements
  • The facility's energy consumption
